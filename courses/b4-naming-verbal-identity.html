Part 1 · covers Brand Algorithm (voice) + Udemy verbal identity

Verbal identity: name, tagline, voice, never-say 6 min live

Verbal identity is not just a logo with words. It is four connected parts: the name you go by, the tagline that carries the promise, the voice that governs how you always speak, and the never-say list that draws the line. Together they are one system - and the most important thing to know about it is that examples teach a model far better than adjectives ever will.

LiveThe four parts, and how they fit together3 min▶

Each part has a distinct job, and each one is a place AI helps you explore:

  • Name - the word people say and search. It carries the most weight and is the hardest to change later.
  • Tagline - one line that carries the positioning as a feeling, not a feature list.
  • Voice - the consistent way the brand speaks across every channel: a set of principles, each shown with a do and a don't.
  • Never-say list - the banned words, claims, and tones. Small list, huge leverage - it removes most off-brand output in one line.
The rule that runs through tonight "Be bold and human" tells an AI nothing it can act on. A do/don't pair - "say your notes are ready, never revolutionize your meetings" - it can follow exactly. Show, do not describe.
Self-studyWhy examples beat adjectives, every time2 min read▶

Adjectives are the sameness trap in disguise. Ten brands all ask AI to be "bold, innovative, and authentic" and get ten versions of the same beige paragraph, because those words describe the average. A model has no shared definition of "warm" - but it can copy a sentence you show it. So a voice guide built on sample lines and a never-say list steers hard off the average; one built on adjectives just rejoins it. This is the whole reason the verbal identity is the highest-value brand doc in the AI age.

Part 2 · covers Udemy naming + the trademark/IP rail

Naming with AI - and the trademark rail 5 min live

AI is a naming machine: it will hand you a hundred candidates in a minute across every style. That speed is the gift and the trap. You still have to screen for meaning and memorability - and you absolutely must check trademark and domain before you commit, because an AI-generated name may already belong to someone else. AI names, AI does not clear them.

LiveThree naming styles, then the check that is not optional3 min▶

Most names fall into one of a few styles. AI can generate all of them on demand - your job is to pick the style that fits the strategy, then screen the output:

  • Descriptive - says what it does ("MeetingNotes AI"). Instantly clear, but weak differentiation and hard to protect.
  • Evocative - a real word borrowed for its feeling ("Cadence" - the rhythm of a good conversation). Memorable and ownable.
  • Invented - a coined word ("Notion", "Zapier"). Maximum trademark room, but you have to spend marketing to give it meaning.
The clearance step is human, and it is a rail Before any name goes further: a trademark search in your market, a domain and social-handle check, and a quick "does this mean something bad in another language" pass. AI can list candidates; it cannot clear them, and shipping an infringing name is a rail-two failure.
Self-studyWhich naming style fits when2 min read▶
Naming typeWhen it fits
Descriptive ("MeetingNotes AI")You need instant clarity and search traffic; you accept weak differentiation and a hard trademark path.
Evocative ("Cadence")You want memorable and ownable, and you have a story to explain the link. Easier to protect.
Invented ("Notion", "Zapier")You want maximum trademark and domain room, and you can invest to give the word meaning.
Compound / blend ("Otterly", "Fireflies")You want a bit of both - a hint of meaning plus room to own it. Watch that it still says easily.

Cadence is evocative: it borrows the rhythm of a well-run conversation, which is exactly what a calm note-taker protects. That is why it survived the shortlist - the meaning matched the strategy, not just the sound.

Part 3 · covers The Brand Algorithm - train AI in your voice

Voice rules + the never-say list 4 min live

Voice is where the AI age rewards you the most. A voice guide is a handful of principles, each shown with a do and a don't, plus a never-say list of banned words, claims, and tones. That never-say list is the highest-leverage line in the entire brand system: it removes most off-brand output before you write a single positive rule.

LiveFour Cadence voice principles, shown not told2 min▶

Cadence is calm, capable, and quietly on your side. Here is that turned into rules a model can follow - each with a do and a don't:

  • Calm, never hyped. Do: "Your notes are ready." Don't: "Revolutionize your meetings!"
  • Plain over clever. Do: "We wrote down what was decided." Don't: "Synergize your action items."
  • On your side, not in your way. Do: "You talk. We will capture it." Don't: "Leverage our AI engine to maximize output."
  • Confident, not boastful. Do: "Every decision, captured." Don't: "The world's most advanced note-taker."
Self-studyThe never-say list does the heavy lifting2 min read▶

A never-say list is short and blunt: the words, claims, and tones the brand refuses. For Cadence that is the hype vocabulary ("revolutionary", "seamless", "game-changer", "unlock", "cutting-edge", "synergy"), the exclamation-heavy tone, and any claim it cannot back up. One line like this removes more off-brand output than pages of positive guidance, because a model finds it far easier to obey a boundary than to interpret an ideal. Ship the never-say list first; refine the positive principles after.

Demo 1 of 2

Generate + screen a Cadence name and tagline shortlist ★ 13 min · everyone builds

Run the naming process end to end: explore at volume with AI, screen for meaning and memorability, then apply the human clearance step that no model can do for you. You walk out with a shortlist you actually trust.

Open any chat AI. Feed it Cadence's positioning and personality from b2-b3 so the names are on-strategy, not random.

Explore: ask for 20 name candidates across all three styles - descriptive, evocative, invented - plus a one-line reason each fits a calm, present note-taker.

Screen: cut to a shortlist of five on meaning and memorability. Say each out loud - if it is hard to say or spell, it is out.

Clear (human, required): for each finalist run a trademark search in your market, a domain and handle check, and a cross-language sense check. Drop anything taken or risky - this is the rail.

Then ask for 5 taglines for your chosen name that carry the promise as a feeling. Screen the same way. This is how Cadence and "Be present. We will take the notes." survived.

★ The naming + screening promptHere is our product: an AI note-taker for consultants and team leads in back-to-back calls. Positioning: notes + decisions are drafted the moment a call ends, so they stay present. Personality: calm, capable, quietly on your side. 1) Give me 20 name candidates across three styles - descriptive, evocative, invented - with a one-line reason each fits a calm, present note-taker. 2) Then shortlist the 5 strongest on meaning and memorability, and flag any that sound generic for the category. Do not claim any name is trademark-free - I will run clearance myself.
The line AI must not cross Never let the model tell you a name is "available" or "safe to use". It has no live trademark data and it does not check. Clearance is a human step with real search tools - treat any AI reassurance as noise.
Demo 2 of 2

Build Cadence's voice guide ★ 8 min · build your own

Now the artifact that pays off for the rest of the course: a compact voice guide - four principles with do/don't examples and a never-say list. This is the exact document that will power the Cadence brand GPT and the consistency simulator in b6, so build it to be used by a model, not admired in a PDF.

Give the AI Cadence's personality words and ask it to draft four voice principles, each with a short DO line and a DON'T line - real sentences, not adjectives.

Ask it to propose a never-say list: banned words, banned claims, and banned tones. Push it to include the hype vocabulary the whole category overuses.

Edit hard. Rewrite every example in Cadence's actual voice - this is rail one, your judgement doing the work the model cannot.

Keep it to one page. Save it as "Cadence voice guide". You will paste this straight into the b6 brand GPT and watch the consistency meter climb because of it.

★ The voice-guide promptBuild a one-page voice guide for Cadence, an AI note-taker. Personality: calm, capable, quietly on your side. It helps people be present in meetings. Produce: 1) Four voice principles. For each, a short DO example sentence and a DON'T example sentence - real copy, not adjectives. 2) A "never say" list: banned words (include category hype like "revolutionary", "seamless", "game-changer"), banned claims, and banned tones. Keep it concrete and copy-ready. Do not describe the voice; show it in examples.
Real world

The six-word list that outperformed a style guide. A team had a beautiful 40-page brand book and their AI content still drifted into hype. They added one never-say line to their prompts - six banned words and "no exclamation-mark selling" - and the drift stopped that week. The boundary a model could obey did more than all the prose it could only skim. That is why your voice guide, not the model, is the thing that keeps Cadence sounding like Cadence.

AI generates names; it does not clear them. Clearance - trademark, domain, cross-language - is a human rail, because shipping an infringing name is a real legal exposure.
