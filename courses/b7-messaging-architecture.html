Part 1 · covers the messaging-hierarchy canon

The message house 6 min live

A message house is the simplest way to keep a brand's story straight across a growing team. One master narrative sits on top - the single most important thing the brand wants understood. Under it stand two to four message pillars, each a distinct reason to believe. Under those sits the proof: real features, outcomes, and customers. Every claim traces up to the narrative, and every claim rests on proof. That is the whole trick - it lets ten people say one true thing in the brand's words.

LiveWhy a house, and not just a list3 min▶

A flat list of talking points drifts fast - everyone grabs a different favourite and the story splinters. A house has structure, and structure is what keeps a growing team aligned:

  • The roof is the one thing. If a customer remembers a single sentence, this is it. For Cadence: you can be fully present, because the notes take care of themselves.
  • Pillars are distinct reasons to believe. Two to four, no overlap. Each is a different angle on the narrative, not a synonym for it.
  • Proof is the load-bearing floor. A pillar with no real proof is a wish, not a message. If you cannot point to a feature, an outcome, or a customer, it does not go in the house.
The trace test Every claim your team makes should trace up to the narrative and down to a proof point. If it floats free of both, it does not belong in the house - and it does not belong in the deck either.
Self-studyTwo to four pillars - why the number matters2 min read▶

One pillar is just the narrative repeated. Five or more and no one can hold them in their head, so the story fragments in exactly the way the house is meant to prevent. Two to four is the sweet spot: enough to be interesting, few enough to stay memorable. For Cadence, three (presence, readiness, privacy) covers the promise without diluting it. Pick pillars that are genuinely different from each other - if two could swap proof points, collapse them into one.

Part 2 · covers Udemy on-brand generation + the messaging canon

Building it with AI, on-system 5 min live

This is where the brand GPT earns its keep. Because it already holds Cadence's positioning, personality, and voice, it drafts a house that sounds like Cadence from the first pass - not the category average. Your job shifts from writing to steering and verifying: shape the pillars, and check that every proof point is true.

LiveDraft with the brand GPT, then tailor per audience3 min▶

Two moves, in order:

  • Draft the core once. Ask the brand GPT for the master narrative and pillars. Because it is on-system, the words already carry the voice - calm, capable, quietly on your side.
  • Tailor without changing the core. The same house, spoken to different people. A solo user hears "be present, notes handle themselves." A team lead hears "your team's decisions never get lost." An exec hears "meeting time turns into shipped decisions." Same narrative, same pillars, same proof - different emphasis and language. You never rewrite the house per audience; you re-express it.
Rail: proof points must be real The brand GPT will happily invent a plausible statistic or a customer quote. It cannot know what is true. Every proof point comes back to you: confirm the feature ships, the number is measured, the customer is real and cleared to name. On-brand and on-system is not the same as true - only you own true.

Part 3 · the reference every team shares

Consistency across the org 4 min live

A message house is only worth building if people use it. Its real job is to be the single reference - the one place sales, support, and marketing all point to - so the brand says one true thing whether it is a sales deck, a help article, or a launch post.

LiveOne house, every team2 min▶

Without a shared house, each team quietly writes its own story and the brand blurs. With one, everyone starts from the same narrative and pillars and adapts to their own moment:

  • Sales pulls the exec pitch and the pillar that fits the deal.
  • Support reaches for the calm, on-your-side voice and the presence pillar.
  • Marketing turns the narrative into campaigns in b8 - without inventing a new story.

The house is the guardrail. It does not make everyone say the identical sentence; it makes sure every sentence is true to the same brand.

Self-studyFeed the house to the brand GPT2 min read▶

The last step closes the loop: paste the finished, verified house back into the brand GPT's instructions (the one you built in b6). Now the GPT does not just know Cadence's voice - it knows Cadence's story, structured. Ask it for a sales one-pager, a support macro, or a launch line, and it draws from the same house every time. That is how a two-person team and a two-hundred-person team stay on-message with the same tool: the reference lives inside the assistant everyone already uses.

Demo 1 of 2

Build Cadence's message house ★ 13 min · everyone builds

Draft the whole house with the brand GPT - master narrative, three pillars, and proof under each - then do the one thing AI cannot: verify the proof is real.

Open the Cadence brand GPT from b6 (the one holding positioning, personality, and voice). If you are between chats, paste your one-page brand system in first.

Ask for the master narrative - the single most important thing Cadence wants understood. Push for one sentence, in Cadence's voice.

Ask for three message pillars under it, each a distinct reason to believe, plus two or three candidate proof points per pillar.

Verify. Go through every proof point and mark it real, needs-a-source, or invented. Delete or fix anything you cannot stand behind. This is the part that is yours, not the model's.

Tighten the pillars so no two overlap. If two share proof, collapse them. Save the verified house - you will feed it back to the brand GPT and reuse it in b8.

★ The message-house prompt (paste to your Cadence brand GPT)Using everything you know about Cadence (positioning, personality, voice), build a message house. 1) Master narrative: the ONE thing a person should understand about Cadence, in one sentence, in our voice. 2) Message pillars: 3 distinct reasons to believe, no overlap. Each in a short phrase. 3) Proof: under each pillar, list 2-3 candidate proof points (a real feature, a measurable outcome, or a customer type). Mark any you are NOT certain is true as [VERIFY]. Stay on-voice: calm, capable, quietly on our side. Never say "revolutionary", "seamless", "game-changer". Do not invent statistics or customer names.
Real world

The pillar that could not stand up. A team let AI draft a house and shipped it whole. One pillar leaned on "trusted by thousands of teams" - a number no one had checked. A journalist asked for the source and there wasn't one. The fix was free and boring: mark every proof point [VERIFY] before it leaves the room. On-brand words are cheap; a claim you can defend is the whole point.

Demo 2 of 2

Audience variants + a boilerplate ★ 8 min · build your own

From one verified house, pour out the reusable pieces: three elevator pitches tuned to different people, and the standard boilerplate paragraph - all tracing to the same narrative, none of them a new story.

Give the brand GPT your verified house and ask for one-line pitches for three audiences: a solo user, a team lead, and an exec.

Check the core held. Each pitch should be the same narrative and pillars in different words - if one invents a new promise, it drifted; send it back.

Ask for a boilerplate paragraph - the "about Cadence" text for a footer, a press note, or an app-store blurb - built straight from the narrative and pillars.

Edit for voice and truth, then keep all four pieces with the house. This is your ready-to-use messaging kit going into activation.

★ The variants + boilerplate promptHere is Cadence's verified message house: [paste narrative + 3 pillars + proof]. Do not change the core. Re-express the SAME narrative and pillars for three audiences: - Solo user: personal presence and calm. - Team lead: decisions never get lost, the team stays aligned. - Exec: meeting time turns into shipped decisions. Give one elevator pitch per audience (one sentence each). Then write one boilerplate paragraph (3-4 sentences) for an "about Cadence" note. Stay on-voice. Use only proof from the house - invent nothing.
Same house, different door If you ever find yourself changing the pillars to fit an audience, stop - you are building a second brand. Tailoring is re-emphasis and re-wording, never a new promise. The house stays put; only the way you walk people through it changes.
Homework

Try it yourself - this week ◐ 30-45 min total

  • Finish Cadence's message house if you did not complete it live - narrative, three pillars, and verified proof under each.
  • Run the [VERIFY] pass properly: for every proof point, write where the proof actually comes from, or delete it.
  • Feed the finished house back into your Cadence brand GPT's instructions, then ask it for a sales one-liner and check it draws from the house.
  • Write the boilerplate paragraph and the three audience pitches. Keep them with the house as your messaging kit.
  • Optional: build a mini house for your own brand or product. Notice which pillar has the weakest proof - that is your next real task.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · What is a message house?

The house is structured on purpose: a roof (the one thing), pillars (distinct reasons to believe), and a proof foundation. Every claim traces up to the narrative and down to proof.

2 · How do you tailor the house for a different audience?

Tailoring is re-emphasis, never a new promise. If you change the pillars per audience, you are quietly building a second brand.

3 · The brand GPT drafts a proof point with a specific customer count. What do you do?

On-brand and on-system is not the same as true. Proof points must be real - every one comes back to you to confirm the feature, number, or customer.
