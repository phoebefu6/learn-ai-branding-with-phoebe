Part 1 · covers Clay 2026 + strategy-house canon

Strategy precedes identity 6 min live

The most common branding mistake is starting with the logo. Identity - name, voice, look - is the designed expression of a strategy. If the strategy is not decided, the identity is decoration with nothing underneath, and AI will happily fill the gap with the average. So we decide strategy first: category, audience, differentiation, purpose, promise. AI helps you research and articulate each one. You decide every one.

LiveWhy the house has to come first3 min▶

The strategy house is the simplest way to hold a brand's foundation in one picture. You build it from the bottom up, and each layer constrains the one above it.

  • Foundation - purpose + values. Why the brand exists beyond making money, and what it will and will not do. Everything rests on this.
  • Pillars. The two-to-four things the brand is consistently about - the proof themes that support the promise.
  • Promise. The single thing you pledge to every customer, every time.
  • Positioning - the roofline. The sharp sentence that places you in a category, for an audience, with a difference nobody else can claim.

Skip this and you get a logo with no meaning - and AI, asked to "brand" you from a thin prompt, will hand you the category average every time. The house is the thing the model has to obey.

The order is the point Strategy, then identity. Decide what Cadence stands for tonight; design how it looks and sounds in b3 through b7. Do it in that order and every later decision gets easier.
Self-studyStrategy vs identity - the clean split2 min read▶

A quick way to tell which is which: strategy is decided, identity is designed.

  • Strategy (this session): category, audience, differentiation, purpose, promise, pillars. Words and decisions.
  • Identity (b3-b7): personality, name, voice, visual direction, guidelines, messaging. The designed expression of the strategy.

Clay's 2026 guide puts it plainly: strategy comes first, identity is its expression, and the strongest brands keep the two aligned as they grow. When you brief AI later, you are really handing it this house and asking it to express it - never to invent it.

Part 2 · covers Clay 2026 + positioning canon

Positioning with AI, off the average 5 min live

Positioning is the sharpest sentence in the house. AI is genuinely useful here - it scans competitors and audiences fast, and it drafts clean statements - but it will drift to category-speak unless you steer it. The job is to end with a difference only Cadence can claim, stated in plain language, backed by facts you have verified.

LiveResearch, then a statement only you could own3 min▶

Two moves. First, research - and treat everything the model returns as a lead to verify, not a fact. Second, a positioning statement in a fixed shape so the difference has nowhere to hide:

★ The positioning shapeFor [audience] who [need or moment], Cadence is the [category] that [single point of difference], unlike [main alternative], because [reason to believe].
  • Audience. Specific enough to picture one person. "Consultants in back-to-back client calls", not "busy professionals".
  • Difference. One thing, ownable, not a feature list. If a competitor could say it too, it is not positioning yet.
  • Reason to believe. The proof that makes the claim credible - and the thing you must verify, not let AI assert.
The category-speak test Read your statement and ask: could three competitors paste their name over "Cadence" and have it still be true? If yes, it is the average. Cut until only Cadence fits.

Part 3 · the meaning layer humans own

Purpose, promise, pillars 4 min live

These three are the meaning of the brand, and rail one says humans own meaning. AI can draft candidate wordings and pressure-test them, but the choice of what Cadence believes and promises is yours - it is the part that cannot be outsourced without becoming the average.

LiveCadence's foundation, in human words2 min▶

Working drafts you will sharpen in the demo - notice each is plain, human, and specific to Cadence:

  • Purpose: so people can be fully present in the room, not scribbling in it. The reason Cadence exists beyond "take notes".
  • Promise: your notes and decisions are ready the moment the call ends. One thing, pledged every time.
  • Pillars: fully present (capture that gets out of your way), trusted notes (accurate, decision-ready), quietly capable (powerful without shouting).
Real world

The purpose that survived the pivot. A software brand changed its product twice in three years but kept one purpose - "give people back their attention". Because the meaning was human-owned and stable, every AI-drafted campaign still sounded like them. The strategy house held while the identity flexed. That is what a decided foundation buys you.

Self-studyValues, and the "we will not" line2 min read▶

Values only matter when they cost you something. A value that no competitor would disagree with ("we value quality") is filler. Write values as trade-offs Cadence is willing to make:

  • Calm over loud. We will not ship attention-grabbing features that break focus, even if they demo well.
  • Accuracy over speed-of-hype. We would rather say "still summarizing" than confidently show a wrong decision.
  • Presence over surveillance. We record to free people, not to monitor them.

These trade-offs become the "never say / never do" seeds you will carry into voice (b4) and governance (b10). A brand is as much what it refuses as what it claims.

Demo 1 of 2

Research, then a positioning statement only Cadence could own ★ 13 min · everyone builds

Three moves: a competitor and audience scan you verify, three genuinely different positioning options, then one you pick and sharpen past the point any rival could claim it.

Open any chat AI. Ask for a scan of the AI note-taker category - who the main players are and how they position. Mark every claim "to verify" and check two or three yourself. AI gives leads, not facts.

Ask for the audience's real jobs-to-be-done and frustrations. Pick the one audience and one moment Cadence serves best.

Ask for three differentiated positioning options - each a different angle, none using category-speak like "AI-powered" or "seamless". Reject any two competitors could share.

Pick one. Run it through the category-speak test: swap in a rival's name - if it still reads true, it is the average. Cut until only Cadence fits.

Lock the final statement in the fixed shape. This is the roofline of your house.

Prompt A - average (drift to category-speak)Write a positioning statement for an AI note-taking app.
★ Prompt B - systematized (research + differentiated options)Act as a brand strategist. We are positioning Cadence, an AI note-taker. 1) Scan the AI note-taker category: main players and how each positions. Label every claim "VERIFY" - do not present guesses as facts. 2) For the audience "consultants and team leads in back-to-back client calls", list their top 3 jobs-to-be-done and top 3 frustrations. 3) Give me 3 DIFFERENT positioning options, each a distinct angle. Ban category-speak: no "AI-powered", "seamless", "revolutionary", "game-changer". Use this shape: "For [audience] who [moment], Cadence is the [category] that [one difference], unlike [alternative], because [reason to believe]." Flag any option a competitor could also claim.
Verify before you believe Competitor claims, market sizes, and "everyone knows" facts are exactly what AI fabricates most confidently. Rail two in miniature: check the reason-to-believe yourself before it enters your brand.
Demo 2 of 2

Fill Cadence's strategy house ★ 8 min · build your own

Now the whole foundation on one page: purpose, promise, and three pillars. AI drafts fast options; you choose the ones that are true and human. This is the artifact b3 through b7 will keep pointing back to.

Give the AI your locked positioning from demo 1 - the house has to sit under that roof.

Ask for three purpose options (why Cadence exists beyond "take notes"), three one-line promise options, and a set of candidate pillars.

Choose one purpose and one promise in your own words. If the AI wording is close but not quite yours, rewrite it - meaning is human-owned.

Pick exactly three pillars. More than four and the brand stands for nothing. Each pillar should be a proof theme, not a feature.

Write it out as a filled house. Keep it - you will hand it to AI as context in every session from b3 on.

★ The strategy-house promptHere is Cadence's locked positioning: [paste your statement]. Draft the rest of the strategy house so it sits under that positioning: - Purpose: 3 options for why Cadence exists beyond "taking notes". Human, plain, no jargon. - Promise: 3 one-line options for the single thing we pledge every customer. - Pillars: 5 candidate pillars (proof themes, not features). I will pick 3. For each, say in one line why it supports the positioning. Do not invent facts. Keep every line something only Cadence would say, not a generic productivity brand.
Real world

Three pillars beat ten. A team asked AI for pillars and shipped all nine it returned - "innovative, trusted, fast, human, secure, simple, bold, caring, smart". It stood for nothing, and every AI-drafted post after that was mush. Cutting to three forced real choices, and the brand got a spine. Constraint is what makes a house hold.

Homework

Try it yourself - this week ◐ 30-45 min total

  • Finish Cadence's positioning statement if you did not lock it live, and run it through the category-speak test with a real competitor's name.
  • Complete the strategy house: one purpose, one promise, exactly three pillars - all in your own words.
  • Verify the reason-to-believe in your positioning. If you cannot back it, soften the claim until you can.
  • Write three values as trade-offs ("X over Y, even when..."), and note the first "never do" seed for b4.
  • Optional: try the same house for your own brand or team. Bring one hard choice to b3.
