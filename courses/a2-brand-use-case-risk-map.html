The brand use-case + risk map: score the bets

Not every brand task is a good place to point AI first. Some are pure upside; some can dilute the brand or walk you into an IP problem. This session gives you one simple grid - value against risk - and a scored shortlist of where to start. By the end you will know your first, safest, highest-value bet, and the order to climb from there, so you lead the adoption instead of chasing whatever the team tried last week.

Part 0

Why you score before you scale

In session a1 you saw where AI helps a brand and where it never should. This session turns that into a decision. Teams tend to point AI at the flashiest task - naming, logos - which are exactly the highest-risk places to start. A leader's job is to sequence: earn trust with high-value, low-risk work first, document the system, and only then climb toward the tasks that carry real IP and dilution risk. One grid does most of the work.

Part 1 · covers Brand Management with GenAI + risk canon

Value vs risk: the scoring grid 6 min live

Score every AI brand use-case on two axes. Value is speed, exploration breadth, and consistency at scale - how much the task gains from an tireless engine. Risk is brand dilution and sameness, IP and likeness and trademark exposure, and how badly it hurts if it goes off-brand at volume. Plot the task, and the quadrant tells you when to touch it.

LiveWhat the two axes really measure3 min▶

Both axes are simple on purpose, so anyone on your team can score a task in a minute.

  • Value = speed + exploration breadth + consistency at scale. A task scores high when AI is genuinely faster, opens up more options to react to, or holds the line across a lot of output. Research and drafting to a system score high; a one-off headline scores low.
  • Risk = dilution + IP + off-brand at scale. A task scores high-risk when it can flatten the brand toward the average, when it touches trademark, copyright, or likeness, or when a mistake ships at volume before a human sees it.
  • The quadrant is the decision. High value plus low risk is where you start. High value plus high risk is worth doing later, with clearance and a human gate. Low value plus high risk is where teams get burned - avoid it.
Questions to ask your team For each place we already use AI, where would it land on this grid? Are we spending our first effort top-left, or did we start with naming and logos because they looked exciting? What is the one task with the best value-to-risk ratio we are not using AI for yet?
Self-studyWhy the risk axis is not optional2 min read▶

Most AI adoption maps only measure value - what is fastest or cheapest. In brand work that is half the picture, and the dangerous half is missing. The risk axis captures the two failure modes that are unique to brand: dilution (the brand quietly drifts toward the category average) and rights exposure (a name, logo, or image lands you in trademark, copyright, or likeness trouble). A task can be high-value and still be the wrong first bet if the risk is high and you have no system or clearance step yet. Score both axes, always.

Real world

The team that started with the logo. A brand pointed AI at a new logo in week one - the exciting, high-risk task - and spent the next month untangling a likeness question and a look that could have been anyone's. A peer brand started with research and consistency audits, documented its voice, and only reached naming in month three, with a clearance step already in place. Same tools, opposite first move, opposite outcome.

Self-studyHow maturity shifts the scores2 min read▶

The grid is not fixed - it moves as your system matures. Content at scale is high-risk with no documented voice, because every asset drifts a different way. The moment you have a real system and a brand GPT, that same task drops toward low-risk, because the steering exists. This is why sequencing matters: building the system early does not just add value, it lowers the risk of everything that comes after it. A leader who understands this stops asking "is AI safe for this task" and starts asking "have we built the thing that makes it safe yet".

Part 3 · the order of the climb

Sequencing: earn trust before you climb 4 min live

The grid tells you where things sit; sequencing tells you the order to touch them. The pattern is the same for almost every brand: start safe and high-value, build the steering, then climb toward the higher-risk tasks with checks in place.

LiveThe three-step climb3 min▶
  • 1 · Start high-value, low-risk. Research, competitor scans, consistency audits, and drafting to a system. These build the team's confidence and skill with AI while the brand is never really at risk.
  • 2 · Document the system first. Before you climb, build the positioning, personality, and voice rules with a "never say" list, and load them into a brand GPT. This is the single move that lowers the risk of everything above it.
  • 3 · Then climb, with IP checks. Naming, visual direction, and content at scale come last, each with a clearance step and a human sign-off. By now the system does the steering and the checks catch the rights.
Questions to ask your team What is our first bet, and is it genuinely top-left? Do we have a documented system yet, or are we climbing without one? For every higher-risk task on our roadmap, who owns the IP-clearance step and the final sign-off before it climbs?
Self-studyThe one mistake that skips the middle step2 min read▶

The most common failure is jumping from step one straight to content at scale without step two. The team gets comfortable with AI on safe tasks, then flips a switch to high-volume output before the system exists. Everything ships fast and everything drifts, because there is no steering. The fix is discipline: treat "document the system" as a gate the roadmap has to pass through, not a nice-to-have you get to later. Speed without the system is just faster dilution.

Leader exercise

Score five use-cases and pick your first bet ◐ 20-30 min

Run the grid on your own brand. The scores turn a vague "we should use AI" into a deliberate first move and a sequence.

  • List five real brand use-cases you are considering for AI - be specific to your brand, not generic.
  • Score each on value (speed, exploration breadth, consistency at scale) and risk (dilution, IP/likeness, off-brand at scale). Plot them on the grid.
  • Pick your first bet: the highest-value, lowest-risk task in the top-left. Name why it is safe to start there today.
  • Sketch the climb: which task comes second, which needs the documented system first, and which needs an IP-clearance step before it can move.
  • Bring your first bet and your riskiest task to session a3, where we go deep on integrity and IP.
Questions to ask your agency or vendor Where are you already using AI on our brand, and where would each of those tasks land on this grid? Which tasks are you treating as safe that actually carry dilution or IP risk? What would you recommend as our first bet, and what would you refuse to touch until we have a documented system?

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · Which is the strongest FIRST bet for AI in brand work?

Top-left tasks are high value and low risk. They build the team's skill and inform everything else while the brand is never really at stake - the safe place to earn trust first.

2 · Why does the grid measure risk and not just value?

A value-only map hides the dangerous half. The risk axis captures brand dilution and rights exposure, so a high-value but high-risk task is not mistaken for a safe first move.

3 · What is the correct sequence for climbing to higher-risk tasks?

Earn trust on safe tasks, build the documented system that lowers the risk of everything above it, then climb toward the higher-risk work with a clearance step and human sign-off in place.
