Part 1 · covers the archetype canon

Archetype + personality 6 min live

The twelve brand archetypes - Sage, Caregiver, Creator, Ruler, Everyman, and the rest - are a shortcut to a consistent personality. Pick one as your backbone and a hundred small decisions get easier, because you can always ask "what would our archetype do here?" From the archetype flow traits, and from traits flows a tone spectrum: where you sit between formal and casual, serious and playful. That spectrum is what drives voice in b4.

LiveWhy an archetype is a shortcut, not a cage3 min▶

An archetype is a well-worn human character - the wise Sage, the nurturing Caregiver, the daring Creator. It gives you a consistent instinct for how the brand behaves, so you are not re-deciding tone from scratch every time.

  • It makes personality consistent. One backbone character keeps a support reply, an ad, and an error message sounding like the same brand.
  • It speeds decisions. "What would a Sage do here?" is faster and more reliable than debating tone by feel.
  • It is a starting point, not a straitjacket. You pick one primary archetype, maybe a secondary flavour, then make it specifically yours with traits and a tone spectrum.
Personality drives voice Everything in b4 - word choice, sentence length, what you never say - is downstream of the character you pick tonight. Decide the who now, and the how-it-speaks gets far easier.
Self-studyTraits and the tone spectrum2 min read▶

From the archetype you draw two things you can actually write with:

  • Traits. Four or five adjectives that describe the character - but only if each comes with behaviour. "Calm" means nothing until you say "we never use exclamation marks in error states".
  • Tone spectrum. A set of dials - formal-casual, serious-playful, reserved-expressive, complex-simple - with a mark showing where you sit on each. A spectrum is more useful than a single label because it captures nuance and gives AI a precise target.

Together the archetype, traits, and spectrum are a character sheet. In b4 you turn that sheet into voice rules and sample copy the AI must obey.

Part 2 · covers archetype canon + Udemy

Choosing Cadence's archetype with AI 5 min live

This is a textbook AI-assists-you-decide moment. AI can explore which archetypes fit your b2 positioning and lay out the trade-offs faster than you could alone. But the choice is judgement - it depends on your audience, your competitors' archetypes, and the brand you actually want to be. AI narrows the field; you pick.

LiveFit against positioning, then a human call3 min▶

Feed the AI your b2 house and ask it to reason from it, not from the average brand. Cadence's positioning - calm presence, decisions ready when the call ends - points somewhere specific:

  • Fit to the promise. A brand that pledges calm and presence is unlikely to be the loud Jester or the aspirational Hero. The Sage (clear, trusted expertise) and Caregiver (quietly on your side) fit far better.
  • Fit to the audience. Consultants in back-to-back calls want competence and calm, not entertainment. That steers the choice.
  • Fit to the whitespace. If every rival is a shouty Hero, a calm Sage stands out - archetype is also a differentiation lever.
Real world

The Sage in a category of Heroes. A calm, explain-it-clearly brand entered a market where every competitor was "unleash your potential". By owning the Sage - measured, trustworthy, never hype - it became the obvious choice for buyers exhausted by the noise. The archetype was the difference, and it was a human who chose it against the grain.

Traits turn into behaviours: a Sage's "measured" means short declarative sentences and no exclamation marks; a Caregiver's "warm" means second-person and reassurance in error states. That behaviour link is what b4 formalizes.

Part 3 · personality shows in behaviour

Tone spectrum + behaviour 4 min live

A personality that lives only in adjectives is decoration. The test is behaviour: what the brand does and does not do in a real moment. So for each tone dimension you place Cadence on the dial and, more importantly, say why - and what that means for how it writes.

LiveWhere Cadence sits, and why2 min▶

Reading the dial from Part 1, each placement is a decision with a reason:

  • Formal-casual: leaning casual, but grounded. Approachable and human, never stiff - but it earns trust, so no slang that undercuts competence.
  • Serious-playful: mostly serious. These are people's real decisions and commitments. A light touch is welcome; jokes at the wrong moment are not.
  • Reserved-expressive: measured. Confident without exclamation marks. The Sage does not shout.
  • Complex-simple: firmly simple. Plain language always. If a sentence needs re-reading, rewrite it. Clarity is the promise made audible.
Adjective, then behaviour Never write a trait without its behaviour. "Calm" is a wish; "calm = short sentences, no exclamation marks, reassurance in errors" is a rule AI can follow and you can enforce.
Self-studyThe do / don't per trait2 min read▶

The cleanest way to make a trait real is a do and a don't. A few for Cadence:

  • Calm - do: state things plainly, one idea per sentence. Don't: pile on exclamation marks or urgency.
  • Trustworthy - do: say "still summarizing" when unsure. Don't: show a confident answer you cannot stand behind.
  • Quietly capable - do: let the result speak. Don't: brag about the technology.
  • Human - do: write to one person, second-person. Don't: hide behind corporate "we are pleased to".

Do this for every trait and you have a character sheet a teammate - or an AI - can act on without asking you. That is the whole point: personality you can hand off without it drifting to the average.

Demo 1 of 2

Map three archetypes to Cadence, then choose ★ 13 min · everyone builds

AI does the exploration - three candidate archetypes mapped to your b2 positioning with honest pros and cons. Then you do the one thing AI cannot: pick, and write down why.

Open any chat AI. Paste Cadence's positioning and strategy house from b2 so the model reasons from your brand, not the average.

Ask for three candidate archetypes that fit the positioning, each with what it would sound like, its pros, and its risks for Cadence.

Ask how each fits the audience and where competitors already sit - archetype is a differentiation lever, not just a vibe.

Choose one primary archetype (and at most one secondary flavour). Write two sentences on why - tie it to the promise and the audience.

Sanity check: would this archetype ever say something your b2 values forbid? If yes, you picked the wrong one. Adjust.

★ Prompt B - systematized (reason from the b2 house)Here is Cadence's positioning and strategy house from our last session: [paste]. Act as a brand strategist. Recommend 3 candidate brand archetypes (from the classic 12) that fit THIS positioning - not a generic app. For each: what it sounds like in one line, 2 pros, 2 risks for Cadence, and how well it fits our audience (consultants in back-to-back calls). Demo 2 of 2

Define traits + tone-spectrum placement ★ 8 min · build your own

Turn the chosen archetype into a working character sheet: four to five traits, each with a do and a don't, and a placement on every tone dial with a one-line reason.

Give the AI your chosen archetype and the b2 house. Ask for five candidate personality traits that flow from both.

For each trait, ask for a concrete do and don't in Cadence's real contexts - onboarding, an error message, a marketing line.

Ask it to place Cadence on four tone dials (formal-casual, serious-playful, reserved-expressive, complex-simple) and justify each in one line.

Edit hard. Cut to four or five traits, rewrite any do/don't that is vague, and adjust the dials until they feel like Cadence, not a generic calm brand.

Save the character sheet. It is the direct input to the voice guide you build in b4.

★ The character-sheet promptCadence's archetype is [your choice], and here is our strategy house: [paste]. Build a brand personality character sheet: 1) 5 candidate personality traits that flow from this archetype and positioning. 2) For each trait, a concrete DO and DON'T in three contexts: onboarding, an error message, a marketing headline. 3) Place Cadence on 4 tone dials - formal-casual, serious-playful, reserved-expressive, complex-simple - with a one-line reason for each placement. Keep it specific to Cadence. No generic "we are innovative and customer-focused".
Real world

The trait that finally had teeth. A brand listed "friendly" for years and every writer interpreted it differently, so the voice wandered. Rewriting it as "friendly = second-person, one contraction per sentence, never corporate 'we are pleased to'" made it enforceable overnight - by people and by their brand GPT. A trait is only real when it tells you what not to do.

Homework

Try it yourself - this week ◐ 30-45 min total

  • Lock Cadence's primary archetype (and optional secondary) with a two-sentence written reason tied to the b2 promise.
  • Finalise four to five personality traits, each with a do and a don't in a real context.
  • Place Cadence on all four tone dials and write one line of reasoning per dial.
  • Write the first three "never say / never do" lines your personality implies - you will fold them into the voice guide in b4.
  • Optional: map your own brand's archetype, and notice which archetype your top competitor occupies.
