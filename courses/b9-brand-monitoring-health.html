Part 0

Why monitoring is where brands quietly drift

A brand does not fall apart in one bad launch. It drifts - a caption here that broke the voice, a partner deck there that used the old promise, a slow slide in how people describe you. Monitoring is how you catch drift while it is still cheap to fix. AI is genuinely great at the reading part: it can chew through a batch of assets or a pile of mentions and summarize. It is not the judge. You supply the data, AI summarizes it, and a human decides what it means and what to do.

Part 1 · covers the listening + perception canon

Two things to watch: perception + consistency 6 min live

Brand health splits cleanly into two questions. Outward: what do people out there think and feel about us (perception, sentiment, share of voice)? Inward: do the assets we ship still match our own system (consistency)? AI helps with both the same way - you supply the data or the assets, AI reads and summarizes at volume, and a human reads the summary and decides. The model is a fast pair of eyes, never the source of truth.

LivePerception and consistency - one loop, two jobs3 min▶

Both jobs run through the same loop; only the input changes.

  • Perception (outward). Feed AI a set of mentions, reviews, or comments you have collected. It clusters themes, reads sentiment, and estimates share of voice. You read the readout and decide if anything is real.
  • Consistency (inward). Feed AI your brand system plus a batch of recent assets. It flags the ones that drifted off voice, broke a never-say rule, or wandered off-message. You adjudicate every flag.
  • Same discipline both ways. You supply the data, AI summarizes, a human reads and decides. Then you refine the system so the next cycle drifts less.
The line that keeps this honest "AI reads it faster than I can; I decide what it means." That sentence is the whole session. It is rail one - humans own the brand soul and the claim - applied to monitoring.
Self-studyWhere the data comes from - you, not the model2 min read▶

AI does not go find mentions for you, and you should not trust it to. It reads what you give it. Collect a real, dated set - exported comments, review text, a spreadsheet of mentions, or a folder of published assets - and paste that in. If you ask a chat AI "what are people saying about my brand" with no data, it will confidently invent a plausible answer. That is the fabrication trap from b1, wearing a monitoring costume. Supply the data, or you are measuring the model's imagination.

Part 2 · covers consistency-audit practice

The consistency audit 5 min live

This is the inward job and the one AI is best at. You feed it the Cadence brand system from b6 plus a batch of assets, and ask it to flag anything off-brand with a reason. It is not deciding; it is surfacing candidates for your eyes. A flag is a "look here", not a verdict.

You ask AI to check......and it flags, with a reason for you to judge
Voice - does it match the voice rules and examples?"This caption is hype-heavy; the brand voice is calm and understated."
Never-say - any banned words, claims, or tones?"Uses 'revolutionary' and 'seamless', both on the never-say list."
On-message - does it carry the positioning?"Sells a generic feature list; the point of difference is missing."
Personality - does it behave like the archetype?"Reads pushy and loud; Cadence is quietly on your side."
Claims - anything factual or comparative it asserts?"Claims 'the fastest note-taker' - a factual claim a human must verify."

Every row ends at a human. AI groups and reasons; you decide whether each flag is a real drift, a false alarm, or a place the guideline itself needs to get sharper.

Part 3 · the guardrails that keep perception honest

Perception + guardrails 4 min live

The outward job - sentiment and share of voice from mentions you supply - is genuinely useful and genuinely easy to over-read. AI can tell you a set of comments skews negative on "pricing". It cannot tell you why, and it certainly cannot tell you what to do about the brand. Perception data assists a brand decision; it never makes one.

LiveRead perception, but keep three guardrails up2 min▶

Perception readouts are only as trustworthy as the discipline around them:

  • Verify the sample. A summary of 40 cherry-picked comments is not the market. Know what you fed in, and say so in the readout.
  • Correlation is not causation. Sentiment dipped the same week you raised prices - maybe related, maybe a coincidence, maybe a competitor's news. AI cannot tell which; do not let it imply it can.
  • Assist, do not decide. A perception dip is an input to a human brand conversation, not an instruction to rebrand. The readout informs; the team judges; a human signs off on any brand move.
Real world

The dashboard that almost forced a rebrand. An AI sentiment tool flagged a sharp negative swing and a team nearly overhauled their voice in a panic. A human looked at the actual mentions: one influential thread about a pricing change, amplified. The brand was fine; the pricing page needed a sentence. Perception data pointed to the right room - then a human decided what was actually in it.

Self-studyShare of voice, carefully2 min read▶

Share of voice - how much of the category conversation is about you versus competitors - is a useful directional read and a terrible precise number. From a supplied set of mentions, AI can estimate roughly how the conversation splits and what themes attach to each brand. Treat it as a compass, not a scoreboard: it tells you which way things are trending in the data you have, not a certified market share. Pair it with the distinctiveness idea from earlier sessions - being talked about is only good if you are being talked about as you, not as a generic option in the category.

Demo 1 of 2

Run a consistency audit of Cadence assets ★ 13 min · everyone builds

Feed AI the Cadence brand system plus three or four assets and let it flag the off-brand ones with reasons. Then do the real work: adjudicate every flag yourself. AI surfaces; you decide.

Gather three or four short Cadence assets - a tagline, a social caption, a sales-email opener, a partner one-liner. Use ones you built earlier or write quick rough versions, and deliberately let one or two drift off-voice.

Paste the Cadence brand system from b6 (positioning, voice rules with examples, never-say list) so AI has something to check against.

Ask AI to audit each asset against the system and flag anything off-brand - voice, never-say, off-message - with a short reason per flag.

Now adjudicate. For each flag decide: real drift (fix the asset), false alarm (AI misread the voice), or guideline gap (the rule was unclear - sharpen it). Write your verdict next to each.

Note which flags AI got wrong. That is your reminder that the audit is a first pass for a human, not a verdict - and that the model is not the source of truth on your own brand.

★ The consistency-audit promptYou are auditing brand assets against a documented system. Use ONLY this system: [paste Cadence positioning, voice rules with do/don't examples, and the never-say list]. Here are the assets to check: 1) [tagline] 2) [social caption] 3) [sales-email opener] 4) [partner one-liner] For each asset, tell me: on-voice? on-message? any never-say violation? Flag anything off-brand with a one-line reason and quote the exact words. Do not rewrite them yet, and do not invent rules that are not in the system. This is a first pass for a human to judge.
Why you adjudicate every flag The audit is only trustworthy because a human closes it. AI is fast and literal; it will flag a deliberate voice choice and miss a subtle off-message line. Your verdicts are what turn a pile of flags into a real health check - and often into a sharper guideline.
Demo 2 of 2

Produce a perception readout from supplied mentions ★ 8 min · build your own

Turn a small, supplied set of mentions into a short readout - themes, sentiment, and honest caveats - then verify it against the raw text. The caveats matter as much as the findings.

Take a small set of Cadence mentions - 12 to 20 short lines of comments, reviews, or reactions. Use a supplied sample for the demo; in real life you export your own, dated.

Ask AI to cluster them into themes, read sentiment per theme, and give a rough share of what the set is talking about - with an explicit note on what the sample can and cannot tell you.

Verify. Open the raw mentions and spot-check two or three themes. Did AI cluster them fairly, or force a pattern that is not really there?

Write the readout as assist-not-decide: findings, caveats, and one question for the team - never a "therefore rebrand". Keep it to what the sample supports.

★ The perception-readout promptHere is a small set of mentions about Cadence [paste 12-20 short lines]. Summarize into a short brand-perception readout: 1) the main themes, grouped; 2) sentiment per theme (positive / mixed / negative); 3) a rough sense of what most of this set is talking about; 4) caveats - what this small, unrepresentative sample can NOT tell us. Base everything only on the lines I gave you. Do not invent mentions or infer causes. End with one question a human brand team should look into - not a recommendation to act.
Real world

The readout that earned trust by admitting its limits. A small brand team started every AI perception summary with a caveat line - "based on 18 comments from one week, not a survey". That single habit stopped executives from over-reading a wobble as a crisis, and made the real signals land harder when they came. Honesty about the sample is what let the readout be useful at all.

Homework

Try it yourself - this week ◐ 30-45 min total

  • Finish the consistency audit if you did not live - and write a verdict next to every single flag. The adjudication is the exercise, not the flagging.
  • Run a consistency audit on a batch of your own real assets against your real brand system. Note the most common kind of drift - that is your guideline's weak spot.
  • Collect a small, dated set of real mentions about a brand you know and run the perception-readout prompt. Then verify it against the raw text and count how many caveats you had to add.
  • Turn one audit finding into a sharper guideline - usually a new never-say word or a clearer voice example. That is the loop closing.
  • Optional: sketch a simple monitoring cadence for Cadence - how often you would audit assets and scan perception, and who reads the readout. You will fold it into governance in b10.

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · What are the two things brand monitoring watches?

Outward: sentiment, share of voice, perception. Inward: are the assets we ship still on-brand? AI reads both at volume; a human decides.

2 · In a consistency audit, what is the AI's job versus the human's?

A flag is a "look here", not a verdict. AI surfaces off-brand candidates fast; you decide real drift vs false alarm vs a guideline that needs sharpening.

3 · A perception scan shows sentiment dropped the week you changed pricing. What is the right move?

Perception data assists a brand decision; it never makes one. Verify what was in the sample, do not assume the dip was caused by the pricing, and keep a human in charge of any brand move.
