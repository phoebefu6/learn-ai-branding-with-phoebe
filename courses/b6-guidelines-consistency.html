Why guidelines are the whole game now

A brand book used to be a PDF nobody opened. In the AI age it is the single most valuable brand asset you own, because it is the thing that steers every AI-generated word and image off the category average and onto your brand. In 2026, documented guidelines - especially verbal, with sample copy and a "never say" list - are a requirement, not a nicety. Part 1 · covers Establishing AI Brand Guidelines + brand-voice training

Guidelines an AI can actually obey 6 min live

Human guidelines say "be confident and warm". An AI cannot use that. Machine-usable guidelines give rules, examples, and hard boundaries: sample copy for each context, explicit "never say" lists, and decision rules. Same brand, written so a model produces it on demand.

LiveThe four things AI needs from your guidelines3 min▶

Translate the human brand book into the parts a model can act on:

  • Positioning + personality in a line each - what Cadence stands for and how it behaves, so the model has a target.
  • Voice rules with sample copy - three or four principles, each with a do and a don't example. Examples teach far better than adjectives.
  • The "never say" list - banned words, claims, and tones. This single list removes most off-brand output.
  • Decision rules - how to handle length, formality by channel, and edge cases, so the model does not improvise your brand.
Real world

The "never say" list that saved a voice. One team's AI content kept drifting into hype - "revolutionary", "seamless", "unlock". Adding a six-word never-say list to their brand GPT did more for consistency than any amount of "be authentic" coaching. Boundaries, not adjectives, are what a model can actually follow.

Self-studyLiving guidelines, not a frozen PDF2 min read▶

Machine-usable guidelines are a living document: version them, add examples as edge cases appear, and keep the brand GPT in sync. A guideline the team cannot find or the GPT does not have is a guideline that does not exist. Store the source of truth somewhere both humans and your tools can reach.

Part 2 · the brand-consistency simulator

Drive it yourself: watch consistency get built ★ 8 min · everyone plays

Here is the whole lesson in one control panel. The AI is generating Cadence brand assets. Every foundation you turn on - positioning, voice + never-say, personality, visual system, guidelines - steers it further off the average. Watch on-brand consistency climb and flip to the scorecard to see how many asset types come out on-brand. Notice what never moves it: a bigger model.

What the simulator is teaching Start with everything off: generic "innovative solutions" sludge that could be anyone. Lock positioning and it gets on-message. Add voice + never-say and the hype words die. Add personality, visual system, and guidelines and every asset type turns on-brand. The system built that consistency - not the model.

Assemble Cadence's machine-usable guidelines ★ 12 min · everyone builds

Pull everything from b2-b5 into the one-page system a model can obey. This is the artifact the brand GPT will run on.

Gather your Cadence outputs so far: positioning (b2), personality + archetype (b3), naming + voice (b4), visual direction (b5).

Ask the AI to compress them into a machine-usable brief: positioning line, personality line, 4 voice rules each with a do/don't example, a never-say list, and 3 decision rules.

Edit hard - especially the never-say list and the examples. These carry the most weight in steering the model.

This one page is your source of truth. It powers the brand GPT in Demo 2 and every content session after.

★ The guidelines-compression promptHere are Cadence's brand foundations: [paste positioning, personality, voice notes, naming, visual direction from b2-b5]. Compress into a one-page, machine-usable brand brief: 1) Positioning in one line; 2) personality in one line; 3) four voice rules, each with a short DO example and a DON'T example; 4) a "never say" list of banned words/claims/tones; 5) three decision rules (length, formality by channel, how to handle edge cases). Keep it concrete - examples over adjectives. Do not add anything not in my inputs.
Demo 2 of 2

Build the Cadence brand GPT ★ 8 min · build your own

Load the system into a reusable assistant so anyone on the team generates on-brand without you in the room. This is how a small brand acts consistent at scale.

Open a custom GPT / project / custom-instructions space in your AI tool. Paste the one-page machine-usable brief from Demo 1 as its standing instructions.

Add the operating rule: "Generate strictly to this brand system. Refuse or flag anything that violates the never-say list. If information is missing, ask rather than invent."

Test it on three asset types - a tagline, a social post, and a sales-email opener. Check each against the system: on-message, on-voice, no banned words.

Compare to a plain chatbot with no system given the same ask. The gap you see is exactly the consistency meter in the simulator, made real.

Save the brand GPT and share it. You just turned a documented system into an enforcement engine - the b8 activation session runs on this.

★ The brand GPT standing instructions (skeleton)You are the Cadence brand assistant. Generate strictly to this system: [paste the one-page machine-usable brief - positioning, personality, voice rules with examples, never-say list, decision rules]. Rules: match the voice examples, obey the never-say list without exception, and keep every claim to what the brief supports. If something needed is missing, ask - do not invent. Flag anything a user requests that would break the brand system.
Real world

How a five-person team sounds like one voice. A startup could not afford a copy chief on every asset, so they built a brand GPT from their system. New hires, contractors, and the founder all ran drafts through it first. The brand held together not because everyone had taste, but because the system did - and the model obeyed it.

Homework
